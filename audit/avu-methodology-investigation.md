# AVU methodology and Gold investigation (source only)

## Why the historical Hash curve steps

`packages/wallet/oracle/energy-basket.ts::avuHashAt` computes each mining entry's USD/hash as sum(price × miner reward / (difficulty × hashesPerDifficulty)), multiplies by hardware hashes/kWh, then averages inverse USD/kWh values using capped market-capitalisation weights. `at` reads the last observation at or before the requested timestamp. This is expected mining revenue per hash, not an observed electricity bill.

`packages/price-feeds/src/historical/index.ts` exposes monthly historical price, difficulty, subsidy and circulation. Bitcoin monthly inputs are means of daily values; other chains are generated by `scripts/build-mined-chains-history.py`. Hardware efficiencies and miner shares are curated dated steps. Latest-at-or-before lookup therefore holds these monthly/curated inputs flat until the next real sample. Difficulty changes are only one cause; rewards, efficiency and weighting also change the result. The curve must not be smoothed into invented observations.

Bitcoin Core estimates network hashrate as chainwork difference / observed block timestamp interval (default 120 blocks), rather than measuring miners' hardware: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bitcoin/master/src/rpc/mining.cpp (`GetNetworkHashPS`). Difficulty retargets every 2016 blocks: https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html . Thus an observed-hashrate estimator needs an explicit matching time window and is affected by block timing randomness.

## Proposed observed-hashrate method — requires owner approval

For each chain and one declared trailing window, compute miner issuance/time (coins/s), multiply by matching USD/coin, divide by that chain's work-estimated hashrate (hashes/s), and sum the resulting revenue/hash for the chains contributing to the existing mining entry. Multiply by efficiency in hashes/kWh, preserving existing inversion and weighting only if those are explicitly retained. Do not sum shared merged-mining hashrates as independent energy inputs. Define subsidy versus fees, miner share, window endpoints, block timestamps/work rules and missing-window behavior before implementation. Match the efficiency assumption: today's fleet estimate versus a dated rated machine are different measurements.

With constant difficulty/reward and the same block window, H ≈ difficulty × hashesPerDifficulty × blocks/s and issuance/s ≈ reward × blocks/s. Their block-rate factors cancel to the present formula. An estimator change alone therefore need not remove steps; finer actual historical input data is a separate requirement. Acceptance should include dimensional analysis, constant-input equivalence, variable-difficulty windows, merged mining and historical coverage, with a versioned methodology and no current-value backfill.

## Spot 0.1263 versus Hash 0.109

0.1263 / 0.109 − 1 ≈ 15.9%. Spot is the wholesale electricity aggregate; Hash is mining revenue converted through hardware efficiency and basket weights. No equation requires equality. The screenshot's Spot age (46h) and Hash age (57min) are not simultaneous observations; Hash age is the oldest market/chain reading used, excluding curated reward/efficiency steps. Without the actual input rows, attribution to a particular market or hardware assumption would be speculation. Compare both at a common historical timestamp and report constituent inputs before decomposing the gap.

## Gold is unfinished, not impossible

There is an existing XAU/USD Chainlink provider: `packages/price-feeds/src/providers/chainlink.ts` maps GOLD/XAU to an Arbitrum feed. However, the chart unconditionally disables Gold, direct oracle assets and checked-in relay symbol configurations omit it, and no dated Gold series is bundled. Annual World Bank averages cannot substitute for dated coin-price comparisons.

Both current Chainlink parsers discard `latestRoundData.updatedAt`: TypeScript stamps `Date.now()`; Rust `providers/chainlink.rs` returns `BTreeMap<String,f64>`, and `collector.rs::price_round` stores collection `now`. A dated implementation must preserve and validate actual round timestamps first. Chainlink's API documents `updatedAt` and historical rounds: https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/api-reference and https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/historical-data .

Bounded implementation proposal: verify canonical XAU feed metadata/decimals and denomination USD/troy ounce; extend the provider/collector contract to carry validated observed timestamps and reject incomplete/stale rounds; configure the feed and store actual dated samples; import historical rounds with phase-aware provenance if long-range history is requested. Enable Gold only where dated feed coverage exists, dividing coin USD price by contemporaneous XAU USD/troy-ounce price. Preserve gaps and disclose available coverage; never fill old dates from the current quote or annual averages. Provider timestamp propagation is a prerequisite separate from the chart UI. No live RPC or owner state was inspected in this investigation.
