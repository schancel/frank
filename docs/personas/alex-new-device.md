# Alex — trying Frank and returning on another device

Fictional working hypothesis; familiarity with Signal or Telegram does not establish demand.

Alex already uses Signal and Telegram and has heard about Stamp. Privacy and crypto sound
interesting, but he has little patience for a technical setup. He tries Frank on a phone,
then wants to use the same account at a desk and understand how to recover access.

His possible motivation is a messaging experience he can trust and return to. Whether he
accepts its setup, payment model, and recovery responsibilities needs observation.

## Normal tasks

- Create an account, understand any costs, and exchange a first message without technical help.
- Follow the available account instructions to open the same account on a second device.
- Find the expected conversations and recognize their send and payment states on both devices.
- Practise the documented recovery flow with a disposable account and available recovery material.

## Observable acceptance on mobile and desktop

- Alex understands what he must keep for recovery and what losing it means, without guessing
  whether a familiar password-reset service exists.
- Both devices identify the same account and conversations without manual network configuration.
- A pending operation stays attributable to the original recipient and amount across devices;
  Alex is not prompted to pay again simply because one view has not caught up.
- Recovery explains what it restored and any remaining limits. A failed restore remains visible
  and does not masquerade as a new empty account or let Alex unknowingly act on incomplete state.
- If the task cannot complete, Alex can describe the problem and the next supported step in
  ordinary language, without understanding relays, RPC, nonces, or coin pools.
